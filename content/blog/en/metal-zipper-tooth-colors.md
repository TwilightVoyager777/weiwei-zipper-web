The color of a metal zipper comes from the finish on its teeth. The five finishes we work with are silver, gold, antique brass, antique bronze (often called gunmetal), and black nickel. Silver and gold are bright and clean; antique brass and antique bronze have an aged, vintage look; black nickel is dark and modern.

Because the teeth on a [metal zipper](/products/metal-zippers) are visible hardware, the finish is often a design decision rather than a technical one. This guide explains what each finish looks like and where it tends to work best.

## Where the color comes from

Each tooth on a metal zipper is a separate piece of metal clamped onto the tape. The base metal is usually a copper alloy such as brass, or aluminum. The color the buyer sees comes from the surface: bright finishes are plated, and antique finishes are darkened to look aged.

Two things follow from this:

* **The finish is a surface.** It looks consistent when new, but on positions that see heavy friction, such as a jean fly or a bag opening, it can soften or wear slightly over a long life. On antique finishes this tends to add character rather than spoil the look.
* **The slider should match.** The slider and pull tab carry their own finish. A zipper looks finished when the slider matches the teeth, or when the contrast between them is deliberate.

## The five finishes

### Silver

A bright, plated silver. It is the most versatile of the five and suits almost any tape color, which makes it the mainstream choice for garments and bags. If you are unsure which finish to choose, silver is the safest starting point.

### Gold

A gold plated finish with a warmer, more premium feel. It is common on bags and on garments positioned at the upper end, and it pairs well with light and neutral tapes, where the warm tone stands out without looking loud.

### Antique brass

A darkened, aged finish with a warm brown tone. It is the classic choice for denim and workwear, and it suits khaki, olive, and indigo tapes. The aged look also hides small marks from wear.

### Antique bronze (gunmetal)

A darker, cooler aged finish that leans toward gunmetal gray. It is understated and works well on dark fabrics and retro styles, such as navy or black workwear. Antique bronze and gunmetal are two names for the same finish in our range.

### Black nickel

A dark, plated finish with a modern, low-key look. It suits black and dark-colored garments and bags where a bright zipper would stand out too much, and it gives a more contemporary feel than the antique finishes.

## Finishes at a glance

| Finish | Look | Common pairings |
|---|---|---|
| Silver | Bright, clean, neutral | Almost any tape; mainstream garments and bags |
| Gold | Warm, bright, premium | Bags, higher-end garments, light tapes |
| Antique brass | Aged, warm brown | Denim, workwear, khaki and indigo tapes |
| Antique bronze (gunmetal) | Aged, cool, dark gray | Dark workwear, retro styles, navy and black tapes |
| Black nickel | Dark, modern, low-key | Black garments and bags, contemporary styles |

## How to choose a finish

**Start with the style.** Vintage and workwear styles usually call for antique brass or antique bronze. Clean, modern styles call for silver or black nickel. Gold is for when the zipper should read as a premium detail.

**Then look at the tape color.** A finish that disappears into the tape makes the zipper quiet; a finish that contrasts with it makes the zipper a feature. Black nickel on a black tape is discreet; gold on the same tape is a statement.

**Keep the whole product consistent.** If the garment or bag already has buttons, rivets, buckles, or snaps, matching the zipper finish to them makes the hardware look like one set.

**Confirm on a sample.** Photos and screens shift color, and a finish can look different next to a particular fabric. Sampling for standard specifications usually takes about 3 days, which is the quickest way to see the real combination.
